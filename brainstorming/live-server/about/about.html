about

as of June 5th, 2026


a bit about this project: the what?, the how?, and most importantly, the why?

philosophy


cuz shit*y teachers exist.


students deserve greater access to information and a way to actually voice their concerns. Tell me how many people know a teacher who's been reported? Now compare that to the never-ending amount of students complaining about shit*y teachers? That's the problem.


trojan gives students a voice, and recognizes the few amazing teachers who rarely get credit by displaying the top-rated ones school-wide.


Our goal isn't to make teachers' lives harder—it's to push the education system forward.

what?


inspired by RateMyTeacher and UWflow, trojan brings course and teacher ratings to high schools. It begins as a pilot at Turner Fenton.


unique features:


  • direct reporting to peel head office (anonymous)
  • monthly teacher updates on their standing + improvement tips from review data
  • automatic weekly scraper to keep data fresh (new staff, salary changes, etc.)
  • test hosting — all past tests in one place
  • built-in AI (i know, buzzwords 🙄): smart summaries, report improvements, and personalized teacher tips
  • much, much, much more

Check out all the features.

why?


nothing complex. Students deserve more control over their school experience. In a business, shareholders vote to steer the direction. Why don't students—the real stakeholders—have that same power?


my wake-up call: a teacher won a coveted Peel District Board award that zero students thought she deserved. If students could vote on awards like these, recipients would actually reflect those who shapes young lives. (She rhymes with "rider", by the way—you know the one.)


Same teacher. I loved the course content—but she made it unbearable. Alumni told me this wasn't new: she'd been toxic for 20 years. If that info had been public, dozens of students could've made informed choices—saving their mental health and university admissions.


And the test hosting? It's simple logic: bad teachers reuse tests out of laziness. If past tests are public, teachers are incentivized to write new ones. Past tests become practice material—not the exact exam you'll write. The job is to teach and create, not copy, paste, and conceal.


so yeah, these are personal issues I had with the bitter—but improvable—education system. Realizing this isn't just my problem, but many students', is what made me go public.


using tech to benefit everyone—not abusing AI to write your lessons while you do nothing (that goes for you, Ms. Snyder).
