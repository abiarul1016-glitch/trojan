about

as of June 5th, 2026


a bit about this project: the what?, the how?, and most importantly, the why?

philosophy


cuz shit*y teachers exist.


students the end-receivers (or the victims, depending on how you see it) of the actions of the education system, deserve greater access to information, while also being able to convey their concerns. tell me how many people do you who have went to report an unfair teacher, relative to the never-ending amount of students who complain about shit*y teachers? not a lot.


trojan prioritizes students access to information, while aiding them to voice their concerns. and for the amazing teachers (there are so many, which sadly, aren't recognized for their efforts), they are rewarded, through the display of the top teachers at school, which are genuinely liked, so that they can hold their rating with pride.


stay optimistic, and pay it forward. ✌🏼

what?


inspired by ratemyteacher, and primarily uwaterloo's uwflow, trojan aims to bring the rating aspect of these platforms to an earlier stage of the education system—highschools. Initially it is planned to begin as a pilot project within turner fenton, so offering much nicher features tailored to the school, which a general platform like RMP cannot deliver, while also offering an intuitive UI to interact with the rich aggregated data.


Furthermore, trojan comes with a suite of features these platforms do not have, making it much more capable, and useful to students:

  • direct course and teacher reporting to peel head office from general inbox (for anonymity purposes)
  • teachers receive update on their standing among students every month, and useful tips on how to improve, based on data from reviews
  • peel head office receives updates as well, regarding the top teachers and bottom teachers at turner, so that they stay in the loop, and are much more informed when awarding certain teachers
  • automatic scraper, which runs every week to keep data accurate, and updated: for example, when new staff are admitted to the school, or get a salary bump
  • allow for user editing—which is then human-verified—so we have access to the richest of data
  • test hosting, so all tests can be found in one place
  • built-in AI (i know buzzwords 🙄): to improve reports to head office, create smart summaries for courses and teachers, and to generate relevant tips to teachers based on real review data
  • much, much, much more

Check out all the features.

why?

nothing complex. Students, the end-receivers of the actions of teachers, deserve much more control, say, and freedom over their experience at school—which many would agree do not get. typically regarding a business, shareholders have some sort of voting power, to steer the direction of the firm, so it better aligns with their aims and values. why do students, the shareholders in this analogy (reasonable considering the increased profitization of schools, for less value) not have this same voting power, and also freedom of access of information? the rating feature of trojan, as well as automatic report generation, aims to bring the control over their education back to students—the way it always should have been.

an opening experience for me regarding this, was when a teacher of mine, won a coveted award from the peel district school board, which the entirety of her student-body would agree, she did not deserve. still she won it. it occured to me that, if students were able to vote for who won such an award, more people would be content with the receiver, and the receipient would likely be representative of someone who has such a great influence on students, and hence the great support. the teacher that won this (rhymes with rider), was not this type of teacher.

another thing, ironically caused by the same teacher, i loved the course i took—the content was great—but the teacher made it unbearable. however, after speaking with many alumni, i came to find out that the treachery was nothing new, and just a worsening of the pattern from 20 years ago. if i was much more aware of this, and her atrocities (crimes against students) were much more public, many, MANY, students would be much more informed on their decision prior to taking such a course, saving a mental health damage, as well as damaging univeristy admission factors that many care about.

lastly, regarding the test hosting features: the goal of trojan is not to make teachers lives harder, or more hater. but rather to encourage them to THEIR JOB from the start. obviously it's not our job as students to dictate to teachers what is their job description, but it is our responsability as students to ensure that we are receiving the best quality education within reasonable means, in exchange for the quarter of life (and the entirety of childhood) that we spend in these institutions. so back to test-sharing. many teachers—often the bad one—reuse tests out of laziness. so the logic behind trojan, is that if tests are much more public than ever, rather than hidden deep within groupchats, or only accesible via connections, so teachers are incentivized to develop new tests, and scrutiny over them is much more possible (everyone is aware the tests are public). then, past tests will serve as practice material—which is what they should be—not the same exact test you will write. the job is to teach and create, not copy, paste, and conceal as best as possible.

so, clearly, a lot of these are the personal issues I've had during my time with the bitter—but improvable—education system. As with many technologies, trojan attempts to be a solution a personal problem of mine. But realizing this isn't just a personal problem, but experienced by many, who rather believe there is nothing they can do, made me go public this.

it serves to be a step forward to improvement: using technologies to benefit everyone—not abusing AI to create your lessons, while you do nothing (that goes for you Ms. Snyder).

stay optimistic—living a life of regret and pessism, is not only hard but torturous. ✌🏼
